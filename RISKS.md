# Risk Register (draft)

Early draft of top risks, captured while scaling the concept in
`PROJECT.md`. This is a head start for the formal Risk Register due at
**Milestone 7** (Week 7, per `CALENDAR.md`) — treat it as a draft to be
formalized (likelihood/impact scoring, owner, status tracking) at that
point, not the final artifact.

| # | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|------|------------|--------|------------|
| 1 | Scope exceeds the 240-hour solo budget — Mesa + OSMnx real roads + a scenario-reading LLM step + LLM negotiation + trying multiple team sizes per scenario + one naive baseline + report generation + full test pyramid + CI, all stacked together | High | High | Strict weekly time-boxing against `CALENDAR.md` hour targets. Predefined cut order if behind: (1) try fewer team sizes per scenario, (2) drop fuzz/property tests, (3) simplify the demo visualization to one still image, (4) write fewer scenarios ahead of time, (5) fall back to a synthetic grid map, (6) shrink scenario scale to small, (7) drop live scenario typing as a demo feature. |
| 2 | Local LLM (Ollama, small model) produces weak/inconsistent natural-language bids, undermining how good the negotiated plans are that the team-size competition scores and the report writes up | Medium | **High → raised** | Keep prompts tightly structured (still NL, but scaffolded). **Decision (2026-08-24): validation is deferred from weeks 2–3 to week 9**, when the GPU server (`HARDWARE.md` Phase 1) exists — the dev laptop (i7-10610U, no GPU, ~3–6 tok/s) was judged not worth prototyping on. **Consequence, accepted knowingly:** the SDD (Milestone 6, week 6) will be written *before* the core premise is empirically validated, so if local bid quality turns out to be poor, it surfaces in week 9 with ~7 weeks left rather than in week 3 with ~13. Contingency if it does: scale scenarios down and/or move to a stronger local model — paid APIs remain out of scope. **Early-warning sign:** GPU server not built by mid-week 8. |
| 3 | OSMnx/real-road-network setup (geocoding, routing, graph cleanup) eats unplanned hours | Medium | Medium | Throwaway **spike in weeks 5–6** (does the city graph load and route?) settles go/no-go while it's cheap; full integration is week 13. Fall back to a synthetic grid graph if the spike fails. Note the spike must respect Nominatim's 1 req/sec limit — geocode once and cache the graph to disk rather than re-fetching. |
| 4 | Local models are the *only* LLM option — paid/hosted APIs are out of scope by constraint, so weak local output can't be bought around | Medium | High | Validate quality early (weeks 2–3 prototype, per risk #2). If local output is too weak, respond by scaling scenarios down or moving to a stronger *local* model, not by adding a paid API. Keep the backend interface abstracted anyway so this stays a one-file change if the constraint ever lifts. |
| 5 | The scenario reader misreads a plain-English scenario (wrong scale, a missing responder type, a nonsensical spread of incidents), producing a broken or unrealistic simulation setup — a particular risk for scenarios typed in live at the demo, since those haven't been checked ahead of time the way the pre-written set has | Medium | Medium | Check the scenario reader's output against an expected structure before it reaches the simulation (see `PROJECT.md` Testing). Clamp/sanity-bound the numbers it produces (incident count, team sizes) to what the project actually supports, rather than trusting the model's numbers directly. For the live demo, rehearse with a couple of pre-tried scenario descriptions and keep a pre-written scenario as a fallback if typing one in live goes wrong. |
| 6 | Trying multiple team sizes per scenario multiplies the total number of runs (team sizes × 5–10 repeats × number of scenarios), and every run needs the local language model — this piles onto the existing slow-local-inference problem (risks #2 and #4) | Medium | Medium | Cap how many team sizes get tried per scenario (cut-order lever #1 in risk #1). Confirm real per-run time at week 9's walking skeleton before locking in 5–10 repeats × several team sizes × several scenarios as a fixed evaluation plan. |
